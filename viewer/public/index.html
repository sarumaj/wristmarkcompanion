How it works

  1. Open WristMarkCompanion on your watch: it shows a marker.
  2. Start the camera here and point it at the watch.
  3. A 3D model appears on the watch. The watch's buttons show the next one.

For a steady model

  • Hold the watch 20 to 50 cm from the camera, its display toward it.
  • Good light helps; avoid glare on the watch's glass.
  • Turn the watch's brightness up. Red light (night mode) works too, a little closer.

Move your wrist

Flick
a quick turn: roll the dice, launch fireworks, make the robot wave
Shake
back and forth: whip up the tornado, stir the campfire, rattle the treasure chest, wring out the rain cloud
Twist
turn about the watch's axis: move the planets a month

The buttons under a model do the same, for when your hands are busy. The first time a model is seen, a picture shows its moves; ? next to its buttons shows them again.

Touch the model

Tap
it reacts: roll the dice, name a planet, startle the fish, fire the balloon's burner
Drag
around the watch: turn the model; the arrow keys do it too

Your other hand in the picture

With the watch held still, e.g. the phone on a stand or a laptop's camera, switch on hand tracking (the hand button, or H) and use your free hand in front of the camera. It runs in your browser too. Your hand hides the model where it is in front of it, and covering the marker keeps the model in place for a few seconds.

Point
at the model with your index finger: like a tap
Pinch
thumb and index on the model, then move: turn it; open to let go
Wave
an open hand: the model reacts as to shaking the watch
Swipe
an open hand across: the watch's next or previous model when connected, else a spin

Steer the watch

Where the Watch button shows: on the watch, open the menu and choose Pair a viewer; here, tap Pair with the watch and hold the watch 15 to 25 cm from the camera. Then the buttons step through its markers, start its cycle or make it buzz. End-to-end encrypted.

No watch at hand?

Open the markers on another screen and point the camera at them.
